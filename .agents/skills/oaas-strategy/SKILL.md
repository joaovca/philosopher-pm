# OaaS Strategy

Evaluate the user's business idea using the AI-enabled services / Outcomes as a Service thesis: companies that use AI to deliver the completed work or final outcome, rather than simply selling software that customers use to do the work themselves.

If essential information is missing, first ask no more than 5 focused questions. Wait for the answers, then provide the evaluation. Use the supplied idea, customer, problem, and intended revenue model. Distinguish evidence from assumptions; do not invent supporting facts. The company examples below illustrate the criteria rather than establish evidence about the user's idea.

Score each of these 8 criteria from 0 to 5:

## 1. Existing budget / outsourcing

Question: Does the customer already pay someone to do this work?

The easier it is to replace an existing provider, the better.

Example: Quanta replaces traditional accounting services.

## 2. Vulnerable market

Question: Do incumbent providers face structural difficulties adopting AI without cannibalizing their business model?

Look for fragmented markets built around billable hours, commissions, or large teams.

Example: Mechanical Orchard targets work traditionally performed by large consulting firms.

## 3. Valuable / critical problem

Question: What happens if the customer does not solve this problem?

The more money, revenue, operations, risk, or time is at stake, the better.

Example: PermitFlow handles permits that can hold up an entire construction project.

## 4. Automatable work

Question: How much of the current cost comes from people performing processes that AI could take over?

Look for research, verification, form filling, phone calls, reconciliation, follow-ups, and repetitive tasks.

Example: SuperDial automates administrative work over the phone.

## 5. Verifiable outcome

Question: Can you objectively determine whether the work was done correctly?

The easier it is to measure quality and detect errors, the greater the potential for autonomy.

Example: Ranger in software testing — a test passed or failed, a bug was reproduced or it was not.

## 6. Order-of-magnitude improvement

Question: Does AI make it possible to be dramatically better along some dimension?

Look for something like 10x faster, much cheaper, much more scalable, or significantly more effective.

Example: Listen Labs conducts research and interviews at a scale that would be impossible for traditional teams.

## 7. Recurrence + flywheel

Question: Does the business improve as it grows?

Look for recurring or repeat revenue, plus data or knowledge that enables customer 1,001 to be served better than customer 1.

Example: Vertice accumulates pricing and negotiation data while managing recurring contracts.

## 8. Favorable regulation

Question: Does regulation create a barrier to entry or a ceiling on automation?

The ideal scenario involves a license that is difficult to obtain, while allowing a small number of specialists to supervise a large volume of automated work.

Example: Numeral turns tax complexity into recurring demand and a competitive barrier.

## Scoring

Assign a score from 0 to 5 for each criterion:

- 0 = completely unfavorable
- 1 = very weak
- 2 = below average
- 3 = reasonable
- 4 = strong
- 5 = exceptional

Maximum total: 40 points.

Interpretation:

- 34–40 = exceptional opportunity under this thesis
- 28–33 = strong opportunity
- 21–27 = promising, but with significant problems
- 14–20 = weak fit
- 0–13 = probably not a good fit for the AI-enabled service thesis

Do not give high scores simply because the idea sounds interesting. Be critical and explain what actually supports each score.

## Response format

1. Summarize the idea in one sentence.
2. Create a table: Criterion | Score 0–5 | Rationale.
3. Give the final score: X/40, with its interpretation.
4. List the 3 greatest strengths.
5. List the 3 biggest problems.
6. Show how the user could modify the idea to increase its score.
7. If one exists, propose a better initial wedge: a more specific, urgent problem that is easier to sell.
8. State the most compelling investor pitch, if there is one.
9. End by clearly stating: “I would invest time investigating this” or “I would not prioritize this idea,” explaining why in a few lines.
